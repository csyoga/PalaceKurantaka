\noindent
The origins of this volume lie in our first meeting with Her Highness
Maharani Dr Pramoda Devi in~2017, when we approached her with a
hypothesis arising from our research: that a historically significant
illustrated yoga manuscript was likely to be held in her collection, and
we sought permission to examine it. Her willingness to meet with us
regularly in the years that followed, and her sustained interest in our
research, made it possible for us to undertake this book project.
Throughout the project, Her Highness has provided invaluable
contributions and overseen every stage of the book's development. We
remain deeply grateful for the time she generously devoted to this
project and, above all, for the trust she has placed in us.

Our visits to the Summer Palace in the years that followed were made all
the more enjoyable by the warmth and helpfulness of Shravan Kumar M. R.
and Raghavendra M. S.. We benefited greatly from the assistance of
Nishith Amarnath, whose encyclopaedic knowledge of the history of the
Royal House of Mysore and its archives was an exceptional resource. Uday
Bhaskar at the Jaganmohan Palace also provided us with insightful and
friendly assistance with the royal art collection.

We are deeply grateful to Prof.\ C.\,S.\ Yogananda and Shivashankar~D.\ at
Sriranga Digital Software Technologies, whose kindness and collegiality
transformed what was an exceptionally complex typesetting
undertaking---across three languages and more than one hundred and forty
images---into a particularly rewarding collaboration. H.\,D.\ Ramesh
Sarkar's photographic skill and professionalism has captured the
artistic beauty of the illustrated folios with exceptional precision,
allowing their remarkable qualities to be shared.

We are especially indebted to Prof.~Dominic Goodall at the École
française d'Extrême-Orient, Pondicherry, for taking the time to read
through our preliminary drafts of \emph{Koraṇṭakapaddhati~II} and
\emph{III} with us in person. His extensive comments and suggestions
substantially improved our edition and translation. Prof.~Alexis
Sanderson and Prof.~James Mallinson also offered perceptive comments on
these \emph{paddhati}s during our visit to Oxford in 2025, which led to
further improvements. The research undertaken with Dr.~Mark Singleton
and Prof.~James Mallinson on the Pune manuscript of the
\emph{Haṭhābhyāsapaddhati} as part of the ERC-funded Haṭha Yoga Project
at SOAS University of London (from 2015 to 2020) laid the groundwork for
much of the research presented here. We remain deeply appreciative of
their contributions to that earlier work.

We owe particular thanks to Dr.~S.\,V.\,B.\,K.\,V.\ Gupta at the Amrita
Vishwa Vidyapeetham (Amaravati campus) for assisting us decipher the
peculiarities of the handwritten script in the \emph{Mysore Yoga
Manuscript}. Dr.~Amol Bankar generously shared with us his knowledge of
the \emph{siddha}, Koraṇṭaka, in the Old Marathi sources.

Finally, we extend our special thanks to the following colleagues and
friends, listed in alphabetical order, whose generosity, encouragement
and assistance have contributed to this project in innumerable ways:
Philippa Asher, Dr~Matthew Clark, Raghu Dharmendra, Agata (Agi) Gazda,
Prof.~Shaman Hatley, N.\,S.\ Harsha, Swapnil Balkrishn Jadhav, Mangesh Shashikant Khopkar, 
Dr.~Elizabeth de Michelis, Dr~Suzanne Newcombe, Prof.~Ulrich Pagel, Dr~Laura
von Ostrowski, Dr.~Caleb Simmons, Eric Sakellaropoulos, R.\,G. Singh, 
Dr.~Norman Sjoman, Dr~Ruth Westoby, and Profs.~Dominik and Dagmar Wujastyk.

It has been our great fortune to have been entrusted with the
opportunity to bring this work to light in collaboration with the Royal
House of Mysore, whose custodians have helped shape both the project and
its presentation.
